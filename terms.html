02How a booking is made

Nothing on this website is an offer capable of acceptance, and filling in the form does not make a booking. The sequence is:

  1. You send us an enquiry, by the booking form, email or telephone.
  2. We reply with availability and a written quotation setting out the estate, the dates, the party, every service you have asked for, and the total price including taxes and levies.
  3. You accept the quotation in writing.
  4. We issue a booking confirmation and an invoice for the deposit.
  5. The contract comes into existence when we send the booking confirmation, and not before.

Where we hold dates for you while you decide, that hold is a courtesy and not a contract. We will tell you how long it lasts.

If we spot an obvious error in a price or a description before the contract is made, we will tell you and re-quote, and you are free to walk away owing us nothing. We will not use this to withdraw from a confirmed booking simply because a rate has since risen.

03What the rate includes

The nightly rate for the estate includes:

  • Exclusive use of the whole property for your party, with nothing locked off.
  • Daily housekeeping, bed linen and towels, and a mid-stay change.
  • Pool and garden maintenance.
  • Utilities and wifi.
  • Our 24-hour concierge line for the whole of your stay.

It does not include:

  • Travel to and from Greece.
  • Travel insurance.
  • Chauffeur, private chef, boats, excursions and other arranged services, which are quoted separately (see §11).
  • Food and drink, except anything specifically listed in your quotation.
  • Taxes and levies set by the state (see §4).

Photographs, drawings and descriptions on this website are there to show you what a property is like. Furniture, planting and equipment change over time. If something matters to you, such as a particular bedroom configuration, step-free access or a working fireplace in August, put it in writing before you book. We will confirm it in writing or tell you we cannot.

04Taxes and levies

Greek accommodation is subject to taxes and levies that are set by the state, change from time to time, and vary by season and by type of accommodation. We show them separately from our own charges so you can see exactly what is ours and what is the government's.

Where a levy is payable by the guest and collected by us, it will be itemised in your quotation at the rate in force on the date of the quotation. If the state changes a rate between your booking and your stay, the rate that applies is the one in force on the dates you stay, and we will pass on the difference either way, without adding anything to it.

07There is no 14-day cooling-off period

You may know that contracts made at a distance usually give a consumer 14 days to change their mind. Accommodation booked for a specific date is one of the exceptions. That exception comes from Article 16(l) of Directive 2011/83/EU, as implemented in Greece through Law 2251/1994, and it exists because we take the dates off the market for you.

So once we have confirmed your booking you cannot withdraw from it for free within 14 days. What you can do is cancel under §9, on the terms set out there. We would rather say this plainly here than have you find it out at the point of cancelling.

08Changes you want to make

Tell us as early as you can and we will do what we can. Changing the number of guests, adding or dropping services, or shifting arrival and departure times within the same booking is usually straightforward and free, subject to the property's capacity.

Moving your dates, or moving to a different estate, is a change we will always try to accommodate. Where the new dates carry a different rate, the difference is payable. Where a change is substantial enough to amount to a new booking, such as a different property in a different season, we will say so before you commit to it, and explain why we are treating it as a cancellation and a rebooking.

If you need to change the name of the lead guest or transfer the booking to somebody else, ask us. We will not withhold agreement unreasonably.

10If we cancel

We will not cancel a confirmed booking except where we genuinely cannot perform it — serious damage to the property, a failure of an essential service that cannot be put right in time, or an event under §16.

If that happens, we will, at your choice:

  • move you to one of the other three estates for the same dates, at no extra cost, and pay the difference if the alternative is more expensive; or
  • move your booking to dates that suit you; or
  • refund everything you have paid us, in full, within 14 days.

Where the cancellation is our fault rather than an event beyond our control, you may also claim reasonable, foreseeable costs you have actually incurred and cannot recover elsewhere, a non-refundable flight being the usual example. Keep the receipts and tell us promptly.

11Chauffeur, chef and experiences

Arrival transfers, the private chef, boats, tables, guides and excursions are arranged for you and quoted separately from the accommodation.

Where we provide a service ourselves, we are responsible for it under these terms. Where we arrange it with an independent supplier, such as a boat operator, a licensed guide or a restaurant, we will tell you at the time that we are doing so, and that supplier's own terms apply to their service. We choose these suppliers with care and we do not take a hidden margin on them.

Cars are subject to availability and to the weather and road conditions on the day. A specific vehicle is only confirmed when we confirm it in writing. Any driving of a hired vehicle by a guest is subject to a separate rental agreement, a valid licence, and that hirer's insurance terms.

To resolve before launch — are you selling packages? This is the most important legal question on this site, and it needs a lawyer, not a template. Under the Package Travel Directive (EU) 2015/2302, implemented in Greece by Ministerial Decision 7397/2018, combining accommodation with carriage of passengers (your airport transfers) — or with car rental, or with other tourist services that are a significant part of the value or are advertised as an essential feature of the trip — can make the seller an organiser of a package.

This site advertises chauffeur transfers, a private chef and curated experiences as essential features. If that makes you an organiser, you take on: mandatory pre-contractual information in a prescribed form, liability for the performance of the whole package including other people's services, price-revision limits, statutory termination rights for the traveller, and the expensive one, compulsory insolvency protection: meaning a bond, guarantee or insurance that refunds travellers and repatriates them if you fail. There is also an intermediate “linked travel arrangement” regime with its own duties.

Get advice on which of the three you are. If you are an organiser, this section has to be rewritten around that regime, the booking flow has to serve the prescribed information form before the contract is made, and the insolvency protection has to be in place. Selling packages without it is an offence, not a technicality.

12Arrival, departure and occupancy

Arrival is from 16:00 and departure by 11:00 (to confirm). We are usually flexible either way when the calendar allows. Ask, and if we can, we will.

The maximum number of people who may stay overnight is the number stated on your booking confirmation, which is the property's licensed capacity. Infants under two in a cot are not counted. Exceeding the stated number without our written agreement is a breach of these terms and, because it affects our licence and our insurance, we may end the stay under §13.

Day visitors are welcome in reasonable numbers. Tell the concierge first, so that nobody is surprised by cars at the gate.

  • Please leave the property in a reasonable state. You are not expected to clean it.

We will tell you what the problem is and give you the chance to put it right first, unless it is too serious for that. In seven years we have never had to use this clause.

14Damage and your responsibility

Please tell us about breakages and accidents when they happen. Nobody has ever been charged more for owning up, and a leak found on Tuesday costs a fraction of one found on Saturday.

You are responsible for loss or damage caused deliberately or negligently by anyone in your party or your visitors, beyond fair wear and tear. Where we charge for it, we charge the actual cost of repair or replacement, evidenced, and we account for it against the security deposit under §6.

Your possessions are your own responsibility. Each property has a safe. We are not liable for valuables left unattended, and your travel insurance is the right home for that risk.

15Our responsibility to you

We are responsible for providing the accommodation and the services we have agreed to provide, with reasonable care and skill. If we fail to, we are responsible for loss you suffer that is a foreseeable result of that failure.

We do not limit or exclude our liability for death or personal injury caused by our negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, for gross negligence or wilful misconduct, or for anything else that Greek law does not permit us to limit.

Subject to that, we are not responsible for:

  • losses that were not foreseeable when the contract was made;
  • business losses, since we let these houses to people on holiday;
  • the acts of independent third-party suppliers, where we told you at the time that we were arranging rather than providing (see §11), beyond our own care in choosing them;
  • temporary failures outside our control, such as a power cut, a water restriction or a public works project next door, although we will always tell you what we know and do what we can, and where a failure materially spoils the stay we will discuss a fair reduction.

Where we are liable, our total liability for a booking will not exceed the total amount you paid us for it, except in the cases in the bold paragraph above, where no limit applies.

16Events beyond anyone's control

Neither of us is in breach of these terms because of an event beyond reasonable control — including fire, flood, earthquake, wildfire, extreme weather, epidemic, war, civil unrest, terrorism, strikes, or an act of a government or public authority that prevents the stay.

If such an event prevents your stay, we will offer you the choices in §10: an alternative estate, alternative dates, or a full refund of what you have paid us. In those circumstances neither of us is liable to the other for anything beyond that. We cannot reimburse your flights, and you cannot be asked to pay for a week you could not take.

17Insurance

We strongly recommend comprehensive travel insurance for everyone in your party, taken out when you book rather than before you travel. It should cover cancellation and curtailment, medical expenses and repatriation, and your possessions.

The properties are insured for the buildings and their contents. That insurance is ours and it does not cover your possessions, your cancellation costs or your medical expenses.

18If something goes wrong

Tell us while you are still there. The concierge line is answered at any hour, and almost everything can be put right the same day if we know about it. A problem raised after you have gone home is one we never had the chance to fix.

If it is not resolved on the spot, write to us at within 28 days of departure. We will acknowledge within 3 working days and give you a substantive answer within 14.

If we cannot settle it between us, you may take the dispute to an approved alternative dispute resolution body. In Greece the Consumer Ombudsman (synigoroskatanaloti.gr) offers free mediation for consumer disputes, and the General Secretariat for Trade and Consumer Protection operates the consumer helpline 1520. If you live in another EU member state, an ADR body in your own country may also be competent. Using ADR is voluntary and does not affect your right to go to court.

20Law and jurisdiction

These terms and your booking are governed by Greek law.

If you are a consumer resident in another EU member state, that choice of law does not deprive you of the protection of the mandatory consumer rules of the country where you live (Article 6 of Regulation (EC) 593/2008, “Rome I”).

The Greek courts have jurisdiction. Again, if you are a consumer, that does not take away your right under Regulation (EU) 1215/2012 (“Brussels I bis”) to bring proceedings in the courts of the country where you live, or your right not to be sued anywhere else.
