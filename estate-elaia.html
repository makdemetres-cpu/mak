The setting

Where it is, and
what that means.

Elaia is the only one of the four on the mainland, and the only one you can drive to from Athens. It sits on a flat shelf of land between a working vineyard and the water, with its own jetty at the end of the garden.

It is deliberately the least dramatic of the houses and the one we send people to when they say they want to do nothing. The water here is a sheltered channel rather than open sea, so it is calm on days when the islands are not.

The house

Inside.

A single-storey house, long and low, with the living rooms in the middle and two bedrooms at each end. Everything is on one level from the parking to the jetty, which makes it the easiest of the four for anyone with a mobility problem, and the only one where that is true.

The vineyard behind is not ours and is worked by the family who own it. They are used to guests and will usually say yes if someone asks to walk the rows.

The grounds

Outside.

The pool sits between the house and the jetty and is heated from April. The tennis court is behind the house, screened by cypress, and is floodlit for the evening because at this latitude nobody wants to play at three in the afternoon.

The jetty takes a boat up to about twelve metres. We can arrange a skipper and a day boat with a few days’ notice; Spetses is fifteen minutes across, and Hydra about an hour.
