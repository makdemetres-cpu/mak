The setting

Where it is, and
what that means.

Kyma is built into the hillside above Agios Lazaros in five stepped levels, which is why it can sleep twelve without anybody feeling they are sharing a house. Each level holds one or two suites and opens level onto its own terrace; nobody walks through anybody else’s space to get anywhere.

The orientation is due west. Every terrace on the property gets the sunset, and in August that is at about eight fifteen, which sets the rhythm of the whole day whether you plan it or not.

The house

Inside.

The lower two levels hold the shared rooms: the great room, the kitchen and dining, the screening room and the spa. The upper three are entirely bedrooms. It is the only one of the four houses where two families can take it together and genuinely not be on top of each other.

The finish is more deliberate here than elsewhere: board-marked concrete, a lot of dark stone, and very little colour. It suits the house and it is not to everyone’s taste, which we would rather say now than have you discover on arrival.

The grounds

Outside.

The pool is sixty metres, following the contour of the slope in two connected runs rather than one straight length. It is heated, and there is a shaded shallow section at the upper end that works for small children.

Below the pool the ground falls away steeply to the coastal path. It is spectacular and it is not fenced, which is the reason this is the one house of the four where we do not accept dogs, and why children need watching outside.
