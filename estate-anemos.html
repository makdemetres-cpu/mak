The setting

Where it is, and
what that means.

Anemos is the closest of the four to a town, and the only one you can reasonably walk home to after dinner. It stands in an olive terrace on the hill behind Naoussa, high enough that the harbour noise arrives as a suggestion rather than a fact.

The trees came with the land and most of them are considerably older than the house. Two of them are in the courtyard because it was easier to build around them, which is the single best decision anyone made here.

The house

Inside.

A courtyard plan, built in three low volumes around a paved centre, in the local idiom rather than against it: thick walls, small openings on the road side, and everything opening inward. It stays noticeably cool without air conditioning until about two in the afternoon.

The kitchen is the largest room in the house and was designed to be cooked in by several people at once, which is what tends to happen.

The grounds

Outside.

The pool sits on the lower terrace, away from the bedrooms, so early swimmers wake nobody. Beside it is the outdoor kitchen, which gets used more than the indoor one from June onwards.

The roof deck is the reason to take this house rather than the others. It catches the north wind that gives the villa its name and is the only place on the property with a clear view of both the harbour and the sunset.
